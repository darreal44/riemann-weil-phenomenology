\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The affine law $-\ln\lambda_{\min}=s(\chi)\mu+b(\chi)$ factorizes the
depth problem into a window-invariant staircase $\Delta(\ell)$ and one
arithmetic speed $s(\chi)$. The measured four-variable map for $s$ on
the Dirichlet family is recorded, the $\zeta$-outlier is isolated as
the pole, and two assertions a derivation must supply are stated:
(S1)~linearity in $\mu$ is the prime-number theorem read on the
radical; (S2)~the prefactor $s(\chi)$ is the Lyapunov exponent of
windowed Fourier restriction into the desert, equal to the per-digit
cost of reading $\gamma_1$ (precision law). Three preregistered
predictions for the missing characters $\chi_{-8},\chi_{-20},\chi_{-23}$
are attached. Nothing is claimed about RH.
\end{abstract}

\section{What is already measured}

On fourteen real primitive characters of conductor $\le24$, at three
windows each~\cite{Depth},
\[
-\ln\lambda_{\min}(Q_{\chi,\mu})\;=\;s(\chi)\,\mu+b(\chi),
\]
affine, with $s$ ranging from $0.46$ ($\chi_{24}^{\mathrm{odd}}$) to
$4.00$ ($\chi_3$). $\zeta$ at a depth-adequate basis gives
$s_\zeta=11.7$, off the Dirichlet family by a factor three at the
boundary of the map. The precision campaign~\cite{LabNB} \S16.3 then
identified $s$ a second time: the MUSIC error on $\gamma_1$ falls as
$e^{-s\mu}$, not $e^{-s\mu/2}$. So $s(\chi)$ is the per-digit cost of
reading the first zero from the primes.

The four-variable map on the Dirichlet family (leave-one-out $4.8\%$,
RMS $6.1\%$; $\zeta$ excluded) is
\[
\hat s \;\approx\;
0.14\cdot\gamma_1^{1.32}\,(\gamma_2-\gamma_1)^{0.45}\,
e^{-0.13 D}\cdot 1.32^{[\mathrm{odd}]},
\]
with $D=\sum_{p\mid q}\log p/(\sqrt p-1)$ the arithmetical mass
removed. Residuals of $\chi_{12}$ ($+14\%$) and $\chi_{13}$ ($-10\%$)
remain open.

\section{The lemma candidate}

\begin{conjecture}[Drilling speed]\label{conj:s}
There is a functional $s$ of the pair $(\chi,\text{window construction})$
such that $\ell_0(\chi,\mu)=s(\chi)\mu+b(\chi)+o(1)$ as $\mu$ grows
through depth-adequate bases. The functional factorizes as
\[
s(\chi)\;=\;\kappa_{\mathrm{win}}\cdot \Lambda(\chi),
\]
where $\kappa_{\mathrm{win}}$ is a window invariant (the same for every
$\chi$) and $\Lambda(\chi)$ is an arithmetic coupling of the desert
$(\gamma_1,\gamma_2-\gamma_1)$, the conductor mass $D$, and the parity.
\end{conjecture}

Together with the universality conjecture for $\Delta(\ell)$ this
reproduces the whole quasi-null ladder from two arithmetic scalars and
one window profile.

\section{What a derivation must supply}

\paragraph{S1. Linearity is the prime-number theorem on the radical.}
Each new prime power $p^k\le\mu$ reinforces the rungs that already
recruited $p$, and $\psi(\mu)\sim\mu$ counts those powers. Hence
$d\ell_0/d\mu$ is $O(1)$, not $O(1/\mu)$ (continuum / Paley--Wiener)
and not $O(\log\mu/\mu)$ (Landau count on $\{\log n\}$). Continuum
analysis of the window cannot produce $s\mu$. The lattice is mandatory.

\paragraph{S2. $s$ is the Lyapunov exponent of the desert.}
Hyper-nullity says $|\hat v_0(\gamma_1)|\ll\sqrt{\lambda_0}$: interior
zeros are cancelled far past the $L^2$ budget, and the residual mass
sits at the band frontier. Differentiating the implicit relation
$Q(v_0)=\lambda_0$ along $\mu$ then yields
\[
s \;=\; -\frac{d}{d\mu}\log\bigl|\hat v_0(\gamma_1)\bigr|
\;+\; o(1),
\]
which is the precision law already measured. Deriving the four-variable
map is then deriving how $(\gamma_1,\text{gap},D,\text{parity})$ set
that logarithmic derivative. The map is the phenomenological shadow of
S2, not a substitute for it.

The $\zeta$-outlier is S1+S2 with an extra rank-one pole. Removing the
pole collapses exactly one direction and leaves the rest of the ladder
intact~\cite{LabNB} \S14.5; $s_\zeta$ is therefore not the Dirichlet
map evaluated at $\gamma_1(\zeta)=14.13$.

\section{Preregistered predictions}

Zeros harvested 1 September 2026 (completed $\Lambda$-function,
bisected; caches $\mathtt{zeros\_chim8.pkl}$,
$\mathtt{zeros\_chi20.pkl}$, $\mathtt{zeros\_chi23.pkl}$). The map
gives
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
$\chi$ & $q$ & $\gamma_1$ & $\gamma_2-\gamma_1$ & $D$ & $\hat s$ \\
\midrule
$\chi_{-8}$  & 8  & 3.576 & 3.858 & 1.673 & $1.47$ \\
$\chi_{-20}$ & 20 & 2.359 & 2.317 & 2.975 & $0.57$ \\
$\chi_{-23}$ & 23 & 2.871 & 1.344 & 0.826 & $0.76$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
A measured $s$ off $\hat s$ by more than $20\%$ on any of the three
kills the four-variable map as stated. $\chi_{-23}$ remains the sharpest
test: small gap, low $D$, odd, class number $3$.

\section{Status}

Conjecture~\ref{conj:s} is the lemma candidate. S1 and S2 are the
proof obligations. The four-variable map is a measurement, not the
lemma. The three predictions above are the cheapest falsifiers.
