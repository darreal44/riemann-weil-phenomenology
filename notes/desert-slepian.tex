\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A pure Slepian estimate on the first gap $(\gamma_1-2\pi/L)_+$
underpredicts $-\ln c_L$ by a factor $2.5$ at $\mu=3$ and $5$--$6$
at $\mu=30$--$50$. The factor moves with $\mu$ and is never $1$.
The additive law $aL(\gamma_1-2\pi/L)_++bL\sum(\mathrm{gap}-2\pi/L)_+$
with $a=1.69$, $b=0.82$ is a local fit, not the sampling constant of
the set. The missing object is Beurling--Landau for a hole
\emph{followed by} a sub-Nyquist stretch --- one set, not two terms.
\end{abstract}

\section{Slepian on the hole alone}
Take $f\in PW_{L/2}$, even. The prolate problem with frequency
support in the desert $[0,\gamma_1]$ has parameter
$c=\tfrac L2(\gamma_1-2\pi/L)_+$. The leakage out of the desert is
\[
1-\lambda_0(c)\sim 4\sqrt{\pi c}\,e^{-2c}.
\]
If later zeros sampled the leakage at Nyquist and independently,
one would have $-\ln c_L\approx 2c=-\ln(1-\lambda_0)$ up to
$\tfrac12\ln c$. That is the ``naive $a=2$''.

Measured against that prediction:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
name & $\mu$ & $2c$ & $-\ln\mathrm{Slep}$ & $-\ln\lambda$ & ratio\\
$\zeta$ & 3 & 9.25 & 6.52 & 16.69 & 2.56\\
$\zeta$ & 11 & 27.6 & 24.3 & 109.3 & 4.49\\
$\chi_5$ & 30 & 16.3 & 13.3 & 68.1 & 5.11\\
$\chi_5$ & 50 & 19.7 & 16.6 & 108.5 & 6.52\\
$\chi_{17}$ & 11 & 2.66 & 0.56 & 4.89 & 8.8\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The hole explains the floor \emph{up to a factor}. The factor is
$2.5$ on the smallest window and climbs. It is not $1.69/2$ either:
writing $-\ln\lambda=a_{\mathrm{eff}}L(\gamma_1-2\pi/L)_+$ gives
$a_{\mathrm{eff}}=1.81$ at $\mu=3$ ($\zeta$) and $4.2$--$5.5$ at
$\chi_5$, $\mu=30$--$50$. So $a\in[1.7,2.1]$ is the $\zeta$ fit on
$\mu=3$--$16$, not a constant of the hole.

\section{Why adding gaps does not repair it}
The first zeros after $\gamma_1$ are themselves wider than
$2\pi/L$. Treating each excess as a new Slepian hole and summing
the exponents assumes that a test function can hide in one gap
without seeing the others. The function that realises the floor
is (to leading exponential) the first prolate of the desert: its
Fourier tail on $[\gamma_1,\gamma_*]$ is fixed, one-sided, and
already correlated with those zeros. The extra cost is the
sampling constant of
\[
\Lambda_{\mathrm{early}}=\{\gamma_k:\gamma_k\le\gamma_*\}
\]
as a single non-uniform set for $PW_{L/2}$, not
$\mathrm{cost}(\mathrm{desert})+\sum\mathrm{cost}(\mathrm{gap}_k)$.

That is why $b$ in the additive fit is $0.82$ rather than $1$,
and why $s_{\mathrm{pred}}$ drifts once $\mu$ leaves the window
where $(a,b)$ were pinned.

\section{What Beurling--Landau ask for}
A discrete set $\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}$ is sampling for
$PW_{\Omega}$ if and only if its exterior density satisfies
$D^-(\Lambda)\ge\Omega/\pi$ (Beurling) and a uniform separation
holds. Here $\Omega=L/2$, so the Nyquist density is $L/(2\pi)$,
spacing $2\pi/L$. On $[0,\gamma_1]$ one has $D^-=0$. On
$[\gamma_1,\gamma_*]$ the density climbs from $0$ to Nyquist
through the measured gaps. The sampling constant of that
union is a classical object (Landau's completeness radius,
Slepian on a multi-band frequency set). It is not computed by
adding two Slepian exponents.

A computable proxy, not used as a theorem: the first prolate
$\psi_0$ of the desert, then $\sum_k|\hat\psi_0(\gamma_k)|^2$.
That sum is the quadratic form of $Q_z$ on one vector. It
reproduces the leading exponential only if $\psi_0$ is already
close to the ground state of $Q$; MUSIC says it is not --- the
ground state is a Slepian of the \emph{whole} early set.

\section*{Status}
Diagnosed. The hole gives the right exponential \emph{shape}
and the wrong constant, by a $\mu$-dependent factor $2.5$--$6$.
The additive $(a,b)$ law is a chart, not Beurling. The missing
analysis is the sampling constant of a hole plus a sub-Nyquist
prefix, as one set. Not RH.
