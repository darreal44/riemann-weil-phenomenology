# Li ↔ RH

Bombieri– Lagarias (1999): the following are equivalent.

1. RH
   (all non-trivial zeros have Re=1/2).
2. λ_n ≥ 0
   for all n ≥ 1.

Idea. λ_n = ∑_ρ [1−(1−1/ρ)^n]. Write ρ=1/2+iγ under RH: each term is
positive enough that the sum is ≥0 (with the usual pairing of
conjugates). Conversely, if some ρ has Re(ρ)≠1/2, then |(1−1/ρ)| can be
>1 and the powers (1−1/ρ)^n make λ_n negative for infinitely many n (or
at least one n, after the precise estimates in their paper).

It is the same logical shape as Weil: positivity of a sequence of tests
versus location of zeros. The tests are not compactly supported in an
interval of length L; they are the moments of log ξ at s=1.

Equivalence is not a proof of either side. A finite prefix λ_1,…,λ_N>0
is the same kind of object as a take at one L: evidence inside a window
of degrees, not RH.
