# How A(v) and P(v) diverge (χ₅)

v = (4, −3, 1)/√26, L = log μ.

    μ     L       A        P        Q      A/L     P/L
    6    1.79   −0.269   −0.362   0.093   −0.150  −0.202
    8    2.08   −0.455   −0.515   0.060   −0.219  −0.248
   16    2.77   −0.828   −0.833   0.0055  −0.299  −0.301
   23    3.14   −0.992   −0.998   0.0060  −0.316  −0.318
   29    3.37   −1.088   −1.097   0.0090  −0.323  −0.326
   37    3.61   −1.183   −1.187   0.0040  −0.328  −0.329

A/L and P/L both drift
toward −1/3. Leading
term is the same:

    A(v) ∼ −L/3 + o(L)
    P(v) ∼ −L/3 + o(L)
    Q    = o(L)

A straight line through
Q(μ) gives
Q ≈ 0.147 − 0.045 L
and would cross 0 at
L≈3.3 (μ≈27). The table
does not: after μ=16, Q
sits in a 0.004–0.009
band while L grows.
The linear model for Q
is the wrong remainder.

What a proof needs is
the next term,

    A + L/3  and  P + L/3,

and a sign on their
difference. Empirically
that difference is a
small positive constant
plus prime-sign noise.
Not derived. Not (∀ μ).
