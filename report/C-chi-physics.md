# Physics of C(χ) = ell / (L|E|)

Slepian on *one* interval of length |E| would give −ln c ∼ L|E|, hence
C ∼ 1. Measured C is 0.08–0.20. The bottom mode does not use the
whole hole.

## Fragmentation

E_L is a union, not an interval. A Paley–Wiener mode of type τ hides
efficiently in one coherent piece (the desert, or one fat gap). Extra
components of E add measure that this single mode cannot occupy: the
prolate of a spray of small intervals has 1−λ₀ much closer to 1, per
unit length, than the prolate of a single interval of the same
measure. So

    C(χ) = (effective length used by v₀) / |E|.

Wide desert (χ₅, γ₁ = 6.65): one dominant piece, C = 0.199, stable
from μ = 38 to 62. Narrow desert (χ₂₉, γ₁ = 1.79): E is a spray,
C = 0.085. χ₁₇ sits in between and still settles (0.21 → 0.12).

That is the same fact as the Gram profile: v₀ sits on one mid-band
pair, not on all of E. C is the tax for counting the unused pieces
in the denominator.

## Why a plateau in μ

As μ grows, ν shrinks and more gaps enter E, so |E| grows. ell grows
almost linearly (the measured s). Their ratio freezes once the
*shape* of Γ in the window is self-similar — Weyl: adding height
adds gaps at the local mean spacing, and the fraction of those gaps
that exceed ν tends to a constant for a given statistics of Γ.
Different L-functions have different low-lying statistics (desert,
first tight pair), hence different plateaus. High zeros do not
renegotiate C; they only feed |E| and ell in proportion.

## What C is not

Not γ₁, not log q, not γ₁ log q (χ₅ and χ₁₇ share γ₁ log q ≈ 10.6
and not C). Not |Imax|/|E| (the largest piece shrinks as a fraction
of E when height is added). Not the Slepian constant of Imax
(ell / (L|Imax|) runs from 0.14 to 4.6).

## What would name it

The participation ratio of v₀ on E: (Σ m_k)² / Σ m_k² restricted to
zeros adjacent to components of E, or equivalently the inverse
number of coherent pieces that the bottom window mode can see.
Until that is a theorem, C(χ) is the measured efficiency of E_L as a
hiding set for one mode of type log μ / 2.

## The L-mode

The bottom eigenvector of the Gram, in the cosine window of length L,
has participation N_eff = 1.3–2.8 and centroid k̄ ≤ 1. It is the
ground state of the window: almost constant-plus-one-harmonic, type
τ = L/2, not a high oscillation.

Its Fourier mass still sits on one mid-band pair (γ*/ν from 2 to 20).
A slow time mode seeing a fast pair is ordinary Paley–Wiener: the
prolate of a short window is smooth, and smoothness is expensive
exactly where Γ has a tight pair inside the type.

C(χ) is the coupling of that unique slow mode to E_L. There is not
a second mode to share the spray of gaps; N_eff never climbs with μ
in the cases above. That is why extra pieces of E dilute C instead
of being used.

## Higher harmonics

Gram of χ₂₉ and χ₁₇ at μ=22, first eight eigenvectors.

Only j=0 is deep (λ = 3.6×10⁻³ and 3.3×10⁻⁶). Already j=1 is O(1)
except χ₁₇ (λ=0.17, ell=1.76, still ten times shallower than v0).
From j=2 on, λ ≥ 1: these are not hiding modes.

Their k̄ climbs (0.5 → 2 → 13 → 20) and each sits on a *different*
pair. The spray of E is sampled, but by modes that do not pay an
exponential price. C(χ) is the efficiency of the unique ground
state. Higher harmonics do not furnish extra C.
