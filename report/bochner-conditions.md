# Conditions of Bochner’s theorem

Need a function
φ:ℝ→ℂ with

    (B1) continuous
    (B2) φ(−t)=¯φ(t)
    (B3) positive definite:
         ∑_{i,j} φ(t_i−t_j) c_i ¯c_j ≥ 0
         for all finite samples
    or, equivalently after
    (B1)+(B2), φ=ˆμ for μ≥0
    finite.

m_Q is real, even,
continuous
(k continuous, cos
continuous). (B1)
and (B2) hold.

(B3) fails: already
the 1-point sample
at a t with
m_Q(t)<0 gives
φ(0_diff)·|c|² =
m_Q(t)|c|² < 0.
No need for a
larger Toeplitz.
α_line<0 *is* the
failure of (B3).

Bochner’s theorem
then says m_Q is
not the Fourier
transform of a
finite positive
measure. That we
already knew from
the sign of C_A^{lo}
at t=0 (χ₅,χ₈,χ₄)
and from t=3.7
(χ₃).

There is no extra
condition to
unlock. Tightening
continuity or
decay of m_Q does
not restore (B3).
A different φ
(restriction of
m_Q to the PW
spectrum of W_L,
or a regularisation
that kills the
dip) would be
another theorem,
not Bochner on
the line.
