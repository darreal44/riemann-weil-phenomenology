# All prime towers, Θ→2I

If every n were already
at y/L=0,

    P(∞) = ∑_n 2 w(n)
         = ∑_p 2 log p · r_p/(1−r_p)
    r_p = χ(p)/√p

Partial sums for χ₅:

    Pmax     sum
       3    −1.38
      11    −0.38
      97    +0.75
     199    +1.30
     499    +3.47
     997    +1.46
    4999    +3.79

There is no finite
limit. ∑ log p / √p
diverges like 2√P;
χ only makes it a
walk, not a number.
So “P → P_∞” is
false once every
prime is allowed to
reach 2I.

What actually happens
as μ grows: a prime p
enters at y/L=1
(θ=0), then y/L drops,
P_p moves toward 2w(p).
Meanwhile A is fed by
D₂ on [0,L], which
saturates. New primes
keep arriving. P is a
process, not a limit.

The −2.25 of n=2,3 and
the −1.40 of the 2–3
towers are finite
because those n are
fixed. They are not
the whole of P.
