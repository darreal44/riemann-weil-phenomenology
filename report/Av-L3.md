# Remainder after the −L/3 term

    μ     A+L/3    P+L/3    A+L/π     Q
    6     0.329    0.235    0.302    0.093
   16     0.096    0.091    0.055    0.0055
   23     0.053    0.047    0.006    0.0060
   29     0.034    0.025   −0.016    0.0090
   37     0.021    0.017   −0.034    0.0040

A+L/3 and P+L/3 both go
to 0 from above. L/π
crosses 0 at μ≈23: the
wrong constant.

Q is then a difference of
two vanishing remainders,
not a difference of two
diverging series. The
sign of Q is the sign of

    (A + L/3) − (P + L/3)

and each parenthesis is
already O(0.02) at μ=37.
A proof (∀ μ) is a proof
that P does not overtake
A in this o(L) term.
The table has P+L/3
always a little below
A+L/3 so far. No rate
is fitted (not 1/L, not
e^{−L/2} on four points).
