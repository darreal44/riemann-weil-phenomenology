# Brauer ovals of Cassini on A − P

An eigenvalue lies in the
union of the ovals

    |z − Q_ii| |z − Q_jj| ≤ r_i r_j.

0 is in the (i,j) oval iff
|Q_ii Q_jj| ≤ r_i r_j.
Stronger than Gershgorin
when the radii are equal
and the centres are close.

## H₂ — the 2-plane

One oval. 0 is *outside*.

| μ | |c₀ c₁| | r₀ r₁ | 0 ∈ oval | λ_min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 3.821×10⁻² | 3.787×10⁻² | no | 7.9×10⁻⁴ |
| 16 | 1.779×10⁻² | 1.778×10⁻² | no | 5.0×10⁻⁵ |
| 22 | 1.748×10⁻² | 1.745×10⁻² | no | 1.2×10⁻⁴ |

The margin |c₀ c₁| − r₀ r₁
is 3×10⁻⁴, 1×10⁻⁵, 3×10⁻⁵ —
the same order as det(H₂).
On a 2×2 with equal off-
diagonals, Brauer *is*
|c₀ c₁| > c₀₁², which is
exactly det H₂ > 0.

So Brauer on H₂ is the
determinant test in
disguise. It certifies
H₂ ≻ 0 because both
centres are positive
and the oval splits
into two loops, each
around a positive
focus, neither
crossing 0.

The left tip of the
left loop sits on
λ_min (the 2×2
spectrum lies on the
boundary of its own
Cassini oval). No extra
room.

## H₃ and Q

μ=16: 3/3 ovals of H₃
contain 0; 25/36 of Q
contain 0. Leftmost
real point of the union
is −0.17 (H₃) and −1.2
(Q). Brauer does not
certify the head or
the full matrix.

## Reading

Brauer beats Gershgorin
exactly on the 2-plane,
and there it coincides
with det > 0. It does
not open a new route
around the determinant.
Gershgorin failed
because it uses r_i,
not r_i r_j; the product
is the area that det
already is.
