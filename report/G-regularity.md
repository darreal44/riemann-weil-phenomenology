# Regularity of G

On (0,L] the
continuous piece
is D₂(y)=
2 e^{-2 s₀ y}/(1−e^{-2y}).
Analytic on
(0,∞). Simple
pole at 0:
D₂∼1/y.

G_reg=D₂−1/y
extends
continuously
through 0
(limit +1/2 or
−1/2). It is
C^∞ on [0,L]
(the expansion
of D₂ at 0 is
1/y plus an
entire series
in y). Even
extension to
[−L,L] is C^0
and even; odd
derivatives
vanish, so the
even extension
is C^∞ on
(−L,L) except
possibly a
kink in odd
derivatives
that are
already zero.

The atom at
±log 2 is a
Dirac. G as a
*distribution*
on [−L,L] is
C^∞ plus a
measure. Pairing
against θ
(continuous) is
fine. Pairing
against a
kernel-PD test
that wants a
function
(Krein/Pólya)
sees a jump in
the primitive,
not a C^∞
function.

Consequences
for rates:
the cosine
coefficients
of G_reg decay
faster than
any polynomial
(analytic on a
neighbourhood
of [−L,L]
after even
extension — the
nearest
singularity of
D₂ is at
y=±iπ, so a
strip of width
π). The atom
contributes
cos(n π log 2
/ L), which
does *not*
decay. That
is the slow
piece of C
in the Schur
block when
χ(2)≠0.

So: G_reg is
as regular as
one wants.
G is not,
because of
T₂. χ₈ and χ₄
have no atom;
their C should
decay fast in
the column
index. χ₅’s C
has a
non-decaying
cosine piece.
That is
visible in
why T₂, not
Hankel, is
the χ₅
obstruction
once C is
measured
honestly.
