# A(v) tracks P(v) as μ grows

v = (4, −3, 1)/√26, χ₅.

    μ      A(v)     P(v)      Q
    8     −0.455   −0.515    0.060
   11     −0.638   −0.666    0.028
   16     −0.828   −0.833    0.0055
   17     −0.857   −0.861    0.0049
   23     −0.992   −0.998    0.0060
   29     −1.088   −1.097    0.0090

Both go to −∞ together
(more of the weight w(y)
and more primes). Q is
the gap, stable at
O(10^{-2}) or smaller.

At μ=p the new prime sits
at y=L, where θ(L)=0, so
it does not enter P(v)
that instant. The drop
between integers is A(μ)
and the existing primes
moving with L, not a
δ-kick from the new p.

The reason v survives
is this tracking, not a
miracle at each prime.
A proof (∀ μ) is a proof
that A(v;μ) − P(v;μ)
stays positive while both
diverge. That is still
open.
