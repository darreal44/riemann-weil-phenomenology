# Spectral radius of A

A = D^{-1/2} Off D^{-1/2} is real symmetric on the computed near block,
so ρ_N = ‖A_near‖₂ = r(A_near). On the infinite tail the lab does not
have r(A). It has an upper bound assembled from ρ_N, ρ_B, ρ_far.

r(A) ≤ ‖A‖ always. If the true r(A_∞)<1 ≤ the assembled ρ, the Neumann
series still converges and the lab cannot see it. That is the same gap
as Frob versus the 2-norm on χ₅: a fatter radius kills a living series.

Computing r(A_∞) is not a finite eigenvalue problem. A finite section of
A gives r(A_N)→r(A_∞) under mild conditions (the operator is a compact
perturbation of a Hankel), but that limit is not a proof that r<1 at a
larger L.

The assembled ρ is the certificate. r(A_∞) is not yet one.

Not Weil.
