# SOS continuation: two channels

μ=3 has two
places: ∞ and 2.
The arithmetic
factor, if it
exists, has two
channels

    T f = (T_∞ f,  T_2 f)
    Q(f) = ‖T_∞ f‖² + ‖T_2 f‖²

not
‖T_∞ f‖² − T_2(f)
(the additive
split that
dies because
Q_∞ ≱ 0).

T_2 f has to
be a vector
whose square
reproduces the
lag, including
the negative
directions of
the matrix T₂
on V₉. A single
scalar
αf+βτf cannot.
A short
windowed
function of y
near log 2
might
(continuous
fibre at 2,
same idea as
∞).

That doubles
the “continuous
fibre” problem:
two integral
kernels, K_∞
and K_2, with

    Q(f) = ∫ K_∞(y) |D_∞ f|²
         + ∫ K_2(y) |D_2 f|²

and K≥0. #57
said the cutoff
symbol of the
*sum* is not
≥0. Two
positive
densities can
still sum to a
form whose
Bochner symbol
dips — they
cannot, if the
sum of two
squares is the
form. So this
writing *is*
the PD
extension
Krein wants,
packaged as
SOS.

Obstruction
already
measured:
λ_min(Q_∞)=
−0.074 and
T₂(v)=Q_∞(v)
on the ground
state at 3×10⁻⁶.
The two
channels are
not independent
squares; they
cancel on v.
A SOS must
entangle ∞
and 2 in the
same row
(a single
vector whose
norm² sees
both), not
two separate
‖·‖².

That
entanglement
is the next
object, not
another
Hankel
constant.
It is already
named in
`sos-arithmetic.tex`
(“mixing”).
