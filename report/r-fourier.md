# Fourier coefficients of r

r
is
C^∞
on
the
compact
[0,L],
so
its
Fourier
coeffs
against
sin(ω_k)
and
cos(ω_k)
are
O(k^{−N})
for
every
N
after
enough
IPP.
The
first
sine
coeff
is
already
measured:

    ∫ r sin(ω_k)
      = −0.00398 / k
      + o(1/k).

That
is
O(1/k),
not
O(1/k²),
because
the
endpoint
r(L)≠0
stops
the
first
IPP
at
order
1.
A
second
IPP
would
gain
a
power
if
r(L)
were
zero;
it
is
not.

These
coeffs
are
not
the
slow
cosine
amps
of
D₂
(those
were
1/y).
They
are
small
and
named.
They
do
not
need
a
new
table.

Not
RH.
