# The partner of the well is not supersymmetric

χ₅. v₀ = ground state, v₁ = first
excited (the “partner” at ℓ₀−12).

## What looks like SUSY

A shift (or centered difference)
on the hat index maps v₀ onto v₁:

| μ | ⟨Δv₀, v₁⟩ | ⟨Δv₀, v₂⟩ | angle(v₀,v₁) in {φ₀,φ₁} |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 0.75 | 0.09 | 89° |
| 16 | 0.80 | 0.00 | 77° |
| 22 | 0.80 | 0.01 | 67° |

Δv₀ ⊥ v₂. On the two lowest
hats the pair is almost a
rotated orthonormal frame
at μ=8, then φ₂ joins and
the angle closes.

That is the same pattern as
a harmonic oscillator or a
Slepian pair (P₀ even, P₁
odd under a discrete
derivative). It is an
*empirical intertwiner on
the hat chain*, not a
factorization of Q.

## What is not SUSY

λ₁/λ₀ = 2.8×10⁴, 2.8×10⁵,
4.3×10⁵ — grows with μ.
A Witten pair would keep
λ₁ = λ₀ (broken only by
the ground energy). Here
the partner is *shallower*
by a factor e^{12}, and
the factor itself runs.

There is no supercharge
Q = A†A, Q̃ = AA† with
A v₀ = 0 and A v₁ living
in a second copy of the
same space. A would have
to raise the hat index
and change the eigenvalue
by e^{12}, which no local
A built from P or A−P
does.

## Reading

Call v₁ the Slepian partner
of the well, not the
supersymmetric partner.
The stable gap Δℓ ≈ 12 is
the even/odd splitting of
the two head-concentrated
modes. It is compatible
with a raising operator on
hats; it is not a SUSY
algebra on Q.
