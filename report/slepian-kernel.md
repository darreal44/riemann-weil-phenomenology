# Slepian kernel versus T

K_nm = ∫_0^Ω η̂_n(g) η̂_m(g) dg
in the hat basis. Ω = γ₁
(desert) or Ω = 2π N / L
(Nyquist of the last hat).
χ₅ μ=16, 13 hats.

## Diagonals

    φ         0     1     2     3     4     5
    K_desert  3.13  3.04  3.03  3.02  2.99  2.91
    K_Nyquist 3.19  3.09  3.09  3.09  3.08  3.08
    Q         0.09  0.19  0.24  3.01  2.30  2.84

The Slepian diagonal is
flat and O(3). Q matches
it only in the tail. The
head of Q has been eaten
by A−P.

## Off-diagonals, first row

    K_desert   3.13  −0.08  −0.08  −0.08  −0.09
    K_Nyquist  3.19  −0.04  −0.04  −0.04  −0.04
    Q          0.09   0.13   0.15   0.08   0.34
    T (φ₃…)    3.01   0.94   0.28  −0.02   0.20

Slepian: small *negative*
sidelobes (Dirichlet).
T: large *positive*
neighbour. Opposite sign,
ten times bigger.

## Distances

    T vs K_desert   relF=0.87  cos=0.51
    T vs K_Nyquist  relF=0.50  cos=0.88
    T vs c I        relF=0.39  cos=0.92
    H₃ vs K_desert  relF=9.7   cos=0.54

T is closer to a multiple
of the identity than to
either Slepian kernel.
The Slepian kernel is
the concentration operator
of the desert. T is the
Weil form on functions
that already used that
concentration. They share
a diagonal scale and
almost nothing else.
