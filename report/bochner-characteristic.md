# Characteristic function (Bochner)

Bochner: a continuous
φ:ℝ→ℂ is positive
definite iff φ=ˆμ
for a finite positive
measure μ.

On W_{log 3} we
wrote

    Q(f) = ∫ ρ(t) m_Q(t) dt
    ρ = |ˆf|² ≥ 0

m_Q is the
*multiplier*, not
a characteristic
function. It would
be one if
m_Q = ˆν for some
ν≥0, i.e. if Q
were already a
square. α_line =
2 inf m_Q is the
obstruction:
m_Q dips below 0,
so it is not ˆν
for a positive ν,
and Bochner does
not grant
Q̂_L ≥ 0.

The characteristic
function of the
*cutoff kernel*
(1_{[0,L]} e^{-2s₀ y}
in y) is exactly
the Laplace piece
inside k(t). That
φ_L(t) is positive
definite as a
function of t
(k≥0 up to the
constant). The
prime 2 subtracts
w₂ cos(t log 2),
which is the
characteristic
function of two
atoms at ±log 2.
Those atoms are
not a positive
measure when
χ(2)=−1, and even
when χ(2)=+1 they
can oversubtract
relative to C_A^{lo}.

So: k is Bochner.
w₂ cos is Bochner
up to sign(χ(2)).
Their sum plus a
negative constant
is not. That is
why the
characteristic-
function test
fails to take
W_{log 3}.
