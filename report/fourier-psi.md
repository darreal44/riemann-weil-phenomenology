# Fourier on the line: Re ψ

Unfolding D₂ back to t
is the explicit formula.
The archimedean piece
is a multiplier

    m_∞(t) = Re ψ(s₀ + i t/2)
             + constants
             (−log π, …).

Measured (no constant
fitted):

    s₀=1/4:  Re ψ(1/4) = −4.23
             (minimum at t=0)
    s₀=3/4:  Re ψ(3/4) = −1.09
             (again at t=0)

m_∞ is negative at low
frequency. That is why
A is indefinite for
χ₅/χ₃ on V₂
(`A-by-chi-mu3.md`).
A multiplier that is
negative on a set of
positive measure cannot
give Â ≥ 0.

The prime 2 adds an
oscillation (schematic)

    m_2(t) ∝ χ(2) (log 2)/√2
             · 2 cos(t log 2).

Q̂ corresponds to
m_∞ − m_2, not to
m_∞. At t=0, cos=1,
χ₅(2)=−1 so −m_2
*lifts* m_∞. Whether
the lift beats −4.23
after the exact
normalisation is a
constant-hunt, not a
theorem. On W_{log 3}
the Fourier transform
cannot leave a
neighbourhood of t=0
(type is small): one
cannot hide in the
region where Re ψ > 0
(t ≳ 10).

So Fourier explains
the picture we already
have. It does not
produce α≥0. Young
failed by dropping
g(0)=0; Fourier shows
why A alone is the
wrong operator for
χ(2)=−1.
