# Riemann’s ξ

    ξ(s) = ½ s(s−1) π^{−s/2} Γ(s/2) ζ(s)

Entire, ξ(s)=ξ(1−s), real on the critical line, zeros exactly the
non-trivial zeros of ζ.

ξ'/ξ is the logarithmic derivative that Weil’s explicit formula pairs
against a test function. The archimedean part of ξ'/ξ is

    1/s + 1/(s−1) − ½ log π + ½ ψ(s/2)

At s=½+it this is the symbol whose cosine transform should be W_∞ for
the trivial character (plus the s(s−1) which is only two poles, not a
ladder).

This repo’s grid never included ζ as a seventh cell (s₀ for Γ(s/2) at
s=½ is 1/4, same as even χ, but the conductor is 1 and there is the
extra 1/s+1/(s−1)). CST for q=1 would be another mismatch to measure,
not a take.

Li coefficients are moments of ξ'/ξ on a circle; already named in the
journal, not recomputed here.

ξ is the completed object. Q is a quadratic form built from ξ'/ξ.
Identifying W_∞ for χ=1 is identifying the Fourier of that display
above, not a new function.
